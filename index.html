“How Much Oil?”: Exponential Consumption vs. Continuous Sunlight

Barnett (1978) / Hu & White

Suppose geologists grossly underestimated Earth’s reserves: suppose the entire Earth (R = 6.4 × 10⁶ m) consisted entirely of crude oil beneath a thin solid crust.

At the 1970 global consumption rate (1.67 × 10¹⁰ barrels/year), an Earthful of oil lasts 4.1 × 10¹¹ years.

However, if consumption grows exponentially at 7% per year (the 1890-1970 historical rate), the entire Earth is drained dry in just 344 years!

Reference: Barnett (1978), featured in Chenming Hu & Richard M. White, Solar Cells: From Basics to Advanced Systems.

1. High Ambient Heat & Roof Temperature (55°C-65°C)

Concrete rooftops in Dhaka and Rajshahi reach 58°C+ in April-May, reducing standard Silicon output by 10%-14%. N-Type TOPCon (-0.29%/°C) and wide-bandgap Perovskite tandems offer superior thermal resilience.

2. Monsoon Humidity (80%-95% RH) & Encapsulation

Bangladesh’s heavy monsoon humidity accelerates moisture ingress. Commercial installations require glass-glass PID-resistant modules, while future perovskite cells require butyl-rubber edge sealing and inorganic CTLs (NiOₓ, SnO₂).

3. Urban Dust Soiling (Dry Winter Season)

From November to March, airborne construction dust in Dhaka and Chattogram can reduce solar yield by 8%-18% unless panels are tilted at ≥15°-21° and cleaned bi-weekly.

4. Agricultural Land Scarcity & High-Efficiency Tandems

Because fertile arable land cannot be sacrificed for ground-mounted solar farms, Bangladesh’s future relies on high-efficiency (>28%) Perovskite/Silicon tandem panels on urban roofs, riverine chars, and floating solar on Kaptai Lake.

Solar Cell Academy: Device Physics & Architectures

Comprehensive guide to photovoltaic working principles and next-generation device structures

STEP 01 | ABSORPTION

Photon Excitation (hν ≥ Eg)

Incident sunlight passes through the transparent front electrode (FTO/ITO). Photons with energy equal to or greater than the absorber bandgap (Eg) excite electrons from the valence band (Ev) to the conduction band (Ec), creating electron-hole pairs.

STEP 02 | SEPARATION

Selective Charge Transport

Staggered band offsets direct electrons down conduction-band steps toward the ETL/front contact while blocking holes, and direct holes up valence-band steps toward the HTL/back contact while blocking electrons.

STEP 03 | COLLECTION

External Circuit & Power

Collected electrons flow through the external load to deliver electrical power (P = V × I) and recombine with holes at the back metal electrode (Au, Ag, or Carbon).

Regular n-i-p Perovskite

Up to 26.1%

FTO / SnO₂ (ETL) / Perovskite / Spiro-OMeTAD (HTL) / Au

Light enters through the n-type ETL. Delivers high Voc, though doped Spiro-OMeTAD requires moisture protection.

Inverted p-i-n Perovskite

Up to 26.0%

ITO / NiOₓ or SAM (HTL) / Perovskite / C₆₀ (ETL) / Ag

Light enters through the p-type HTL. Offers superior operational stability and low parasitic absorption for tandem top cells.

CTL-Free Architecture

Cost-Effective

FTO / Perovskite Absorber / Printable Carbon

Eliminates expensive organic ETL/HTL layers by matching contact work functions directly to the absorber bands.

Graded Multi-Absorber

Cascade Transport

FTO / Cs₂TiBr₆ / MASnI₃ / Cs₃Bi₂I₉ / Contact

Cascaded band edges allow wide-bandgap outer absorbers to simultaneously absorb photons and act as selective carrier-blocking layers.

2-Terminal (2T) Tandem

Up to 33.9%

Top Cell / Recombination Layer / Bottom Cell

Monolithic series connection: voltages add (Voc,1 + Voc,2), while sub-cell currents must be matched (min(Jtop, Jbot)).

4-Terminal (4T) Tandem

No Jsc Matching

Semi-Transparent Top Cell + Independent Bottom Cell

Mechanically stacked with independent electrical terminals, eliminating current-matching constraints across varying solar spectra.
